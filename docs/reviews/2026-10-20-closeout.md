The follow-up investigations are separate from those original fixes:

- [#844](https://github.com/MrAliev/local-ai-toolkit/issues/844) and [#851](https://github.com/MrAliev/local-ai-toolkit/issues/851): bounded Windows fixture cleanup and retained diagnostics, delivered through PR #852. This does not identify the owner of the original transient image lock.
- [#848](https://github.com/MrAliev/local-ai-toolkit/issues/848): compiler triage is reconciled with deterministic source diagnostics in PR #856; malformed-format handling was subsequently tightened in #861 / PR #864.
- [#843](https://github.com/MrAliev/local-ai-toolkit/issues/843): the original Windows CI null-exit anomaly was not reproduced. Its historical cause remains unknown. Failure artifacts retain command identity, timeout/cancellation and bounded output. A demonstrated allowlist defect was corrected: an unknown exit, timeout or cancellation cannot be excused as a completed nonzero exit.

The #843 verification on 2026-10-21 used isolated `@sourcegraph/scip-typescript@0.4.0`, Node `v24.20.0`, npm `11.19.0` and a child-process-only PATH. All 20 independent real-shim runs executed one passing test, with no skips or retries and the original five-second probe budget. All 13 focused diagnostic tests passed, including seven new interrupted/unknown-exit cases. These results support closing a bounded, non-reproduced investigation; they do not establish that the historical failure's cause was fixed. `scip-python@0.6.6` remains explicitly excluded from the Windows CI prerequisites because of its separately documented upstream startup failure.

Full-suite counts and exact-head CI results are recorded in the individual verification PRs. The roadmap closeout follows the merge of [PR #868 verifying #843](https://github.com/MrAliev/local-ai-toolkit/pull/868). No release or shared-runtime activation is part of this record.
